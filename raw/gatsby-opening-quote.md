# The Great Gatsby — opening narration (source note)

**Work:** The Great Gatsby, F. Scott Fitzgerald, 1925. Public domain in the US
since 2021-01-01 (95 years after publication).

**Passage (Chapter 1, Nick Carraway's narration):**

> "In my younger and more vulnerable years my father gave me some advice that
> I've been turning over in my mind ever since.
>
> 'Whenever you feel like criticizing anyone,' he told me, 'just remember that
> all the people in this world haven't had the advantages that you've had.'
>
> [...] I was inclined to reserve all judgments [...]"

**Context in this vault:** cited as the literary articulation of the
*reference-class* problem in gradable claims (see
wiki/contradictions/thousand-euro-lot-of-money-example.md). Nick's father names
exactly the hidden parameter: the speaker's biography sets the baseline against
which terms like "a lot," "reasonable," or "lazy" are judged. Nick's
"reserve all judgments" is an epistemic policy: reconstruct the other's frame
before adjudicating their claims — the human analogue of triage step 0.
